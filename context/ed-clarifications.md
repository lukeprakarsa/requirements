## #786: Can 2000-Level Courses satisfy the 1000 Level Technical Elective Requirement?

- Category: Undergraduate – Requirements
- Asked by: Anonymous, about Oct 22, 2026 (shown as "3 weeks ago" on Nov 12)
- Answered by: Kathi Fisler (DUS/DMS); the accepted answer
- Captured: Nov 12, 2026, from Luke's screenshot

> **Question:** Or does the Technical Elective Requirement only allow using 1000-level courses?
>
> **Answer:** If those 2000-level courses are also Technical, then yes.

What it settles: a 2000-level CSCI course can fill a CSCI Technical Elective slot unless it is a
non-technical course. Bulletin footnote 3 on that slot: "None of these can be CS non-technical
courses. The list of these is in the concentration handbook."

## #257: Repeated Courses in Requirements 1

- Category: Assignments – Requirements 1
- Asked by: a student, about Oct 23, 2026 (shown as "3 weeks ago" on Nov 13)
- Answered by: Shriram Krishnamurthi (staff); the accepted answer
- Captured: Nov 13, 2026, from Luke's screenshot

> **Question:** For courses with suffixes, we are told to ignore the suffix for any courses that
> have them. However, that may cause duplicate courses to appear in our data. How should that be
> accounted for? Should the duplicate be removed, and if not would the duplicate be considered
> an unnecessary course?
>
> **Answer:** Count it only once. Once the suffixes disappear, all the courses merge into one (in
> our model, not in reality).

What it settles: names that are the same course once trimmed (`"CSCI 2951A"`, `"CSCI 2951B"`)
are one course. It counts once and is never unnecessary. It also means a suffix can't tell two
courses apart, so `"CSCI 1951I"` is the CSCI 1951A of the Handbook's exclusive pairs.

## #321: Another Course Suffixes Question

- Category: Assignments – Requirements 2
- Asked by: a student, about Nov 11, 2026 (shown as "Yesterday" on Nov 12)
- Answered by: Shriram Krishnamurthi (staff)
- Captured: Nov 12, 2026, from Luke's screenshot

> **Question:** I think I asked a similar question to this one in Requirements 1, but I didn't
> make it very clear.
>
> When considering courses such as ENGN 1931J, which is listed as being among the outside-CS
> courses that can still count toward the Sc.B. concentration, should our default assumption be
> that all ENGN 1931 courses should *not* count toward any requirements, or that all ENGN 1931
> courses *should* count toward requirements? One of the reasons I'm using this example is that
> the concentration handbook *specifically says* that ENGN 1931C, ENGN 1931Q and ENGN 1931W all
> cannot be used to satisfy any requirements, even though ENGN 1931J can, meaning that if our
> implementations allowed all ENGN 1931s to be counted toward a requirement, they would directly
> violate something written in the handbook.
>
> Thanks, and sorry this is rather repetitive.
>
> **Answer:** Good question! Count them as in.

What it settles: once suffixes are ignored, a course number counts as approved if the Handbook
approves any suffixed version of it (ENGN 1931, APMA 1930, APMA 2822, IAPA 1701, IAPA 1801,
PLCY 1702), even where the Handbook denies other suffixes. Luke (Nov 12) applies the same answer
to the suffixed CSCI non-technical courses (1952B, 1952X, 2402C, 2952S, 2999A): they count as
technical, as the Req 1 handout says ("treat all of them as technical").
